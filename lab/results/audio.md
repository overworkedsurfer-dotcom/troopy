## UPTEMPO beat sync

Offset = audible kick onset minus the moment the sim crosses the beat; **positive = kick late**. Every even beat is a scheduled kick.

**Does drift grow?** No: within each session the offset is flat (slopes -0.1, —, — ms/min). UPTEMPO advances its kick times by sim (wall) time from one anchor and re-anchors at 45 ms of error, so a skewed audio clock *would* make it ramp; none was skewed enough here. (While this lab was being built, under heavier load from other jobs in the same container, two sessions did drift: +16.3 and +39.8 ms/min, matching measured audio-clock skews of -271 and -665 ppm almost exactly. That is the mechanism above in action, and on a skewed device the kick would creep until the game's 45 ms re-anchor.)

**Where the offset comes from.** The tap also logs every `frequency.setValueAtTime(165 Hz, when)` call (that is how UPTEMPO starts each kick), so the time the game *asked* for is known:

- **render path, audible minus asked: 0.0 / 0.0 / 0.0 ms**, constant. It equals the calibrated look-ahead of Chromium's DynamicsCompressor on UPTEMPO's master bus (5.93 ms). The game does not compensate for it.
- **game placement, asked minus sim beat (heard clock): 0.0 / 0.0 / 0.0 ms**, the part that varies between sessions. UPTEMPO maps sim time to audio time with one anchor `(currentTime, S.t)` taken when audio starts. `currentTime` moves in ~9-9 ms steps and lags by a further ~15 ms during the first blocks after a context starts, so where that one anchor lands is luck, and it sticks for the whole session. The game also subtracts `outputLatency` (32 ms here), while the measured render-to-speaker gap (from `getOutputTimestamp`) was 40 / 41 / 40 ms.

Reference point: all offsets are relative to the *sim* beat. The beat line is drawn at the next animation frame and reaches a real screen a frame or more later, so the visual crossing lags the sim too; the screen side is not measured here.

Method:

1. Every animation frame, right after the game's own rAF callback, the tap logs `rAF time`, `S.t`, `carry` (from `__game.snapshot()`), `S.beat`, `S.bpm`, `S.freeze`, plus `ctx.currentTime` and `ctx.getOutputTimestamp()`. `getState()` exposes `beat`, `bpm`, `mpb` and `t`; `carry` is the only extra needed to place the sim on the wall clock.
2. Each integer beat crossing is interpolated between frames in sim time, then mapped to wall time with `wall = frameTs - (S.t + carry - simCross)` (the real-time loop accumulates exactly the rAF deltas). Even beats are the scheduled kicks: steps 0 and 8 of every bar carry a kick in all seven groove levels (beat 0 is never scheduled by the game).
3. Wall time -> audio clock. "Heard" uses a linear fit of `getOutputTimestamp()` (contextTime vs performanceTime): the context time reaching the speaker at that wall moment. UPTEMPO's own comment says it aims for the kick to be *heard* on the beat, so this is the headline. "Render" uses `currentTime` (upper envelope of its quantisation steps): the context time being rendered.
4. Kick onsets come from the captured signal (sample-exact context timestamps): zero-phase 110 Hz lowpass for detection, then re-timed on a 400 Hz lowpass (first sample above 30 % of the attack peak). Each scheduled kick is matched to the nearest onset within ±min(60 ms, 0.45 of a 16th).
5. Calibration (same run, same browser): a page schedules the game's own kick/bass/hat/snare synth at known context times. Capture timestamps are **sample-exact** (8/8 impulses at 0 frames error); the kick detector's bias is -0.02 ms (worst deviation 0.30 ms), subtracted above. Routing the same kicks through UPTEMPO's master chain (16 kHz lowpass, DynamicsCompressor) shifts them by 5.93 ms; that is Chromium's compressor look-ahead, a real audible delay, so it is **not** subtracted.
6. Drift: least-squares slope of offset vs time within each session; "grows" means the fitted change over the session exceeds 5 ms with r² > 0.3. The audio-clock skew is the slope of the `getOutputTimestamp` fit minus 1.

## Method

- **Capture.** `lab/audio/tap.js` is injected with `addInitScript`. It subclasses `AudioContext` (registers every context, including a second one), patches `AudioNode.prototype.connect` so any connection to a context's `destination` is also teed into a recorder in that context, and patches `disconnect` to follow. Recorder: tap gain -> `DelayNode` (0.25 s, covers the async worklet load so the first sounds are not lost) -> `AudioWorkletNode` loaded from a Blob URL, which downmixes the 2-channel speaker mix to mono and stamps each block with `currentFrame`; ScriptProcessorNode is the fallback. Chromium's `currentFrame` sometimes repeats the previous block's value inside `process()` (47 times in this run), so the recorder keeps its own frame counter; missing frames across all captures: 0. Node pulls the samples every 2 s as base64 Float32.
- **Browser.** Playwright's bundled headless Chromium (headless shell) with `--autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required` and `--mute-audio` (`lab/lib.js` launch). The audio clock **does advance in real time** in headless mode (a fake output device at 44100 Hz, `outputLatency` 32 ms, `baseLatency` 10 ms), so no xvfb or headed mode was needed. Muting the output device does not stop the render graph, and the tap records before the destination, so capture is unaffected by `--mute-audio`.
- **Play.** Real-time mode only (`__game.reset()` is never called). One key unlocks audio, then a per-game pattern (`lab/audio/players.js`) runs: fire/turn (KICKBACK), steer to orbs + dash (LOOPBACK), shift/drop/tilt (TILTWELL), steer to letters + cast (SPELLSNAKE), roll at enemies + pick cards (PIPWALK), walls/GO/pulse/pick (MAZEWRIGHT), buy/move/place/reroll (COMPOUND), sweep aim + fire (JUNK ORBIT), chase + flip polarity (MAGSUMO). UPTEMPO is played by a timing bot that reads `getState().notes`, converts each note to the wall-clock moment it crosses the hit line and presses its lane key then. If a session has not ended by 55 % of its time, the pattern turns sloppy (stops steering, drops blindly, drifts out of the ring...) so the game can end on its own terms and its game-over sound is captured; UPTEMPO keeps playing well throughout. After a game over the runner waits 0.9 s and presses Space to restart.
- **Artifacts.** `lab/results/audio/<id>.wav`: 16-bit PCM mono, resampled to 22.05 kHz with a windowed-sinc filter, first 40 s. `<id>.png` (1200x300, drawn on a canvas in the same browser): level panel with per-pixel peak and 50 ms RMS on a dBFS scale (-60 dB dashed), strips for key presses and onsets (yellow), for UPTEMPO also scheduled beats (cyan, also as lines in the level panel) and detected low-frequency kicks (magenta), then a log-frequency spectrogram 40 Hz-11 kHz (-90..0 dB).

## Limits

- Measures presence, cleanliness and timing, **not quality**. A game can pass every check and still sound harsh, thin or repetitive.
- One short session per game (~25 s) with a scripted, not human, input pattern: later difficulty levels, rare events and long-session fatigue are mostly not heard. Silence % depends on how busy the pattern keeps the game.
- The recorder sees the float mix before the destination. Real devices clip at ±1 the same way, but OS mixers, Bluetooth latency and speaker response are outside the lab. Output latency in headless Chromium is a fake device's estimate, so the "heard" sync numbers describe Chromium's own latency model, not a measured speaker.
- Onset counts are a proxy for "discrete sound events": a sound with two attacks can count twice, and overlapping sounds within 60 ms count once.
- NaN detection was checked with a buffer full of NaNs: they reach the tap, but the tap's DelayNode interpolates, so each NaN sample is counted about twice. Values above ±1 pass through unclamped, so "clip %" sees overs.
- This container was shared with other agents running headless browsers (load ~1.5-2 on 4 cores). Real-time capture is unaffected (sample-exact timestamps, no holes), but audio-clock rate and quantisation in the UPTEMPO sessions vary with that load.
- Multiple contexts are each captured (tested with two contexts at 44.1 and 48 kHz) and mixed by wall-clock alignment, good to ~20 ms; every game here created exactly one.
