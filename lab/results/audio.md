**What this verifies:** that each game makes sound, that the signal is clean (no clipping, no NaN, no DC, no dead air) and, for UPTEMPO, that the kick lands on the beat. **What it does not verify:** whether anything sounds *good*: mix balance, tone, annoyance, loudness over a long session or on real speakers. A human still has to listen to the WAVs.

## UPTEMPO beat sync

Matched 7 of 8 scheduled kicks (88 %). Offset = audible kick onset minus the moment the sim crosses the beat (positive = kick late).

| clock | n | mean ms | median ms | p95 abs ms | p5..p95 ms | sd ms |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| heard (getOutputTimestamp, includes output latency) | 7 | 4.40 | 4.39 | 4.46 | 4.3 .. 4.5 | 0.06 |
| render (ctx.currentTime) | 7 | -36.54 | -36.54 | 36.72 | -36.7 .. -36.3 | 0.14 |

Drift: slope 0.68 ms/min over 7.7 s (r² 0.222); mean |offset| first third 4.37 ms vs last third 4.41 ms. **Drift grows: no.** Consecutive-kick jumps > 15 ms: 0.

Method:

1. Every animation frame, right after the game's own rAF callback, the tap logs `rAF time`, `S.t`, `carry` (from `__game.snapshot()`), `S.beat`, `S.bpm`, `S.freeze`, plus `ctx.currentTime` and `ctx.getOutputTimestamp()`. `getState()` exposes `beat`, `bpm`, `mpb` and `t`; `carry` is the only extra needed to place the sim on the wall clock.
2. Each integer beat crossing is interpolated between frames in sim time, then mapped to wall time with `wall = frameTs - (S.t + carry - simCross)` (the real-time loop accumulates exactly the rAF deltas). Even beats are the scheduled kicks: steps 0 and 8 of every bar carry a kick in all seven groove levels (beat 0 is never scheduled by the game).
3. Wall time -> audio clock: "heard" uses a linear fit of `getOutputTimestamp()` (contextTime vs performanceTime): the context time that is reaching the speaker at that wall moment. "render" uses `currentTime` (upper envelope of its 8.7 ms quantisation): the context time being rendered. They differ by 41.3 ms here; UPTEMPO subtracts `outputLatency` (32 ms in this browser) when it schedules, aiming at the heard clock.
4. Kick onsets come from the captured signal (sample-exact context timestamps): zero-phase 110 Hz lowpass for detection, then re-timed on a 400 Hz lowpass (first sample above 30 % of the attack peak). Each scheduled kick is matched to the nearest onset within ±min(60 ms, 0.45 of a 16th).
5. Calibration (same run, same browser): a page schedules the game's own kick/bass/hat/snare synth at known context times. Capture timestamps are **sample-exact** (8/8 impulses at 0 frames error); the kick detector's bias is -0.03 ms (worst deviation 0.30 ms), subtracted above. Routing the same kicks through UPTEMPO's master chain (16 kHz lowpass, DynamicsCompressor) shifts them by 5.93 ms; that is Chromium's compressor look-ahead, a real audible delay, so it is **not** subtracted.

## Method

- **Capture.** `lab/audio/tap.js` is injected with `addInitScript`. It subclasses `AudioContext` (registers every context, including a second one), patches `AudioNode.prototype.connect` so any connection to a context's `destination` is also teed into a recorder in that context, and patches `disconnect` to follow. Recorder: tap gain -> `DelayNode` (0.25 s, covers the async worklet load so the first sounds are not lost) -> `AudioWorkletNode` loaded from a Blob URL, which downmixes the 2-channel speaker mix to mono and stamps each block with `currentFrame`; ScriptProcessorNode is the fallback. Node pulls the samples every 2 s as base64 Float32.
- **Browser.** Playwright's bundled headless Chromium (headless shell) with `--autoplay-policy=no-user-gesture-required` and `--mute-audio` (`lab/lib.js` launch). The audio clock **does advance in real time** in headless mode (a fake output device at 44100 Hz, `outputLatency` 32 ms, `baseLatency` 10 ms), so no xvfb or headed mode was needed. Muting the output device does not stop the render graph, and the tap records before the destination, so capture is unaffected by `--mute-audio`.
- **Play.** Real-time mode only (`__game.reset()` is never called). One key unlocks audio, then a per-game pattern (`lab/audio/players.js`) runs: fire/turn (KICKBACK), steer to orbs + dash (LOOPBACK), shift/drop/tilt (TILTWELL), steer to letters + cast (SPELLSNAKE), roll at enemies + pick cards (PIPWALK), walls/GO/pulse/pick (MAZEWRIGHT), buy/move/place/reroll (COMPOUND), sweep aim + fire (JUNK ORBIT), chase + flip polarity (MAGSUMO). UPTEMPO is played by a timing bot that reads `getState().notes`, converts each note to the wall-clock moment it crosses the hit line and presses its lane key then. After a game over the runner waits 0.9 s and presses Space to restart.
- **Artifacts.** `lab/results/audio/<id>.wav`: 16-bit PCM mono, resampled to 22.05 kHz with a windowed-sinc filter, first 40 s. `<id>.png`: waveform (min/max) with RMS envelope and key-press ticks, onset strip (yellow), for UPTEMPO scheduled beats (cyan) and detected kicks (magenta), log-frequency spectrogram 40 Hz-11 kHz (-90..0 dB).

## Limits

- Measures presence, cleanliness and timing, **not quality**. A game can pass every check and still sound harsh, thin or repetitive.
- One short session per game (~10 s) with a scripted, not human, input pattern: later difficulty levels, rare events and long-session fatigue are mostly not heard. Silence % depends on how busy the pattern keeps the game.
- The recorder sees the float mix before the destination. Real devices clip at ±1 the same way, but OS mixers, Bluetooth latency and speaker response are outside the lab. Output latency in headless Chromium is a fake device's estimate, so the "heard" sync numbers describe Chromium's own latency model, not a measured speaker.
- Onset counts are a proxy for "discrete sound events": a sound with two attacks can count twice, and overlapping sounds within 60 ms count once.
- Multiple contexts are mixed by wall-clock alignment (a few ms accuracy); every game here created exactly one.
